A first return, within reach.

I’m exploring a table-tennis robot around an SO-101 arm and a simpler camera setup. The first goal is a repeatable return from a slow, controlled feed into a small hitting zone.

The design work brings together camera placement, 3D ball tracking, flight prediction, and arm timing. The practical question is whether the arm, with a paddle attached, can reach the predicted contact point before the ball arrives.

Design work so far

  • A focused physical layout. Two fixed cameras watch overlapping views of the incoming flight and hitting zone, with the arm clamped beside the table and a lightweight paddle mount.
  • A predictive controller to build first. Detect and triangulate the ball, estimate its motion, and select a reachable interception with enough time for a restricted swing and recovery.
  • A path toward learned control. A later simulator could combine measured arm response with ball and contact dynamics before exploring a learned return policy.

Current stage. This is a system design and feasibility project. The physical setup, tracking software, controller, and successful returns are still TBD.

What comes next

Measure the paddle-loaded arm’s reach and motion, calibrate the cameras, and record sensing and actuation delays. Use those measurements to choose a feasible feed speed, then work toward one repeatable return before expanding the playing zone or training a policy.
